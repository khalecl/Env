The scenario · read once

March 2027 — Kariba at 38 percent

Lake Kariba has fallen to 38 percent of usable storage. ZESCO loses 250 MW of firm hydro for an expected six months. The shortfall is replaced by SAPP spot imports at a price well above the hydro it displaces.

Every decision that follows uses this one situation. Nothing new is introduced.

Hours per month720

Exchange rateZMW 26 = US$1
Freshness. These are teaching values for a hypothetical 2027, not published ERB or ZESCO data. The schedule actually in force is the reverted MYTF, 1 November 2025 to 31 October 2027, energy-only with no fixed or maximum-demand charges. Decision 1 of 5
1:30

The revenue gap

What we are deciding. How much extra money ZESCO must collect over six months, and what that costs each unit sold.

ΔC = P_lost × H × (c_import − c_hydro)
P_lost = lost capacity, MW · H = hours per month · c = US$/MWh
Answer
US$9.0m per month → US$54m → US$10.00/MWh
250 × 720 × 50 = 9,000,000
54,000,000 ÷ 5,400,000 MWh = 10.00
✓ 5.4m MWh × $10.00 = $54.0m — reconciles

In Kwacha at 26 to the dollar that is ZMW 0.26/kWh.

Large in dollars, small per unit, because it is spread across a full year of sales. That spreading is the entire economic function of a true-up. Compress recovery into three months and the same gap becomes US$40/MWh, which is politically unpayable. Duration is the lever, not the amount.

Which way does the FTR point?

What we are deciding. Whether a financial transmission right on the Zimbabwe to South Africa corridor is worth buying, or whether the money sits in the opposite direction.

2027 nodal outlook · US$/MWh
Normal · 40%ZW 58 · SA 62
Dry · 35%ZW 95 · SA 65
Wet · 25%ZW 44 · SA 58
E[spread] = Σ πᵢ (p_SA,ᵢ − p_ZW,ᵢ)
AZimbabwe → South Africa
BSouth Africa → Zimbabwe
Answer
−US$5.40/MWh. Auction the reverse: SA → ZW, worth US$4.73m a year
0.40(4) + 0.35(−30) + 0.25(14) = −5.40
100 × 8,760 × 5.40 = 4,730,400
✓ probabilities sum to 1.00 — valid distribution

Hydro scarcity inverts the corridor. The dry state dominates the expectation, and in that state Zimbabwe's own price spikes above South Africa's. An FTR issued on the historical trade direction hedges the wrong risk entirely.

Decision 3 of 5
1:30

Build now, or wait?

What we are deciding. Commit the Kariba–Livingstone uprate from 800 to 1,200 MW at US$80m in 2027, or defer the decision to 2032.

Two equally likely futures
Slow growth · 50%−US$40m
Fast growth · 50%+US$200m
Wait, then slowDo not build · 0
Wait, then fastBuild 2032 · 90% of value
Answer
US$80m now against US$90m waiting — option value US$10m
now: 0.5(−40) + 0.5(200) = 80
wait: 0.5(0) + 0.5(180) = 90
✓ waiting removes $40m downside at 50% (+$20m), costs 10% of $200m at 50% (−$10m) = +$10m

Both strategies pass a conventional NPV test. Waiting still wins, because the right not to build has value whenever the downside is real and uncertainty resolves with time. The recommendation flips if the delay penalty exceeds 20 percent, or if the slow-growth downside is milder than −US$40m. Name the assumption; a board will test it.

Decision 4 of 5
1:30

Set the trigger

What we are deciding. The automatic, formula-driven adjustment that removes the need for an emergency ERB hearing the next time this happens.

Proposed band table · baseline ZMW 1.50/kWh
Storage ≥ 55%0%
45 – 54.9%+5%
35 – 44.9%+10%
Below 35%+15% hard cap
Answer
ZMW 1.65/kWh
1.50 × 1.10 = 1.65
✓ +ZMW 0.15 is 10% of 1.50, below the +15% cap

Recovery. Revert one band at the next quarterly review, once storage has held above the band's upper threshold for two consecutive months. The persistence rule stops the tariff oscillating on a single wet week. Any answer containing a measurement window, a persistence requirement and automatic reversion without a hearing is correct.

Stepped bands beat a continuous formula here for one reason: customers can read a band table. Predictability is a design objective in its own right, not a compromise.

Decision 5 of 5
1:30

Order the reforms, 2027 to 2032

What we are deciding. The sequence. Tap the four in order, first to last.

Answer
D → C → B → A

D first. Everything downstream prices something. Without a defensible transmission revenue requirement and asset base, loss factors, wheeling charges and trigger baselines all rest on an unverified number, and the first legal challenge collapses them.

C second. Loss factors and MW-km wheeling turn the cost base into locational signals and make open access chargeable. B third — the trigger needs a stable baseline to adjust from.

A last. FTRs hedge congestion price differences that must first exist, be measured and be published. That needs zonal or nodal pricing, a functioning day-ahead market and a creditworthy settlement counterparty. SAPP is not there yet, so an FTR market launched in 2027 would have nothing to price.

Also accept D → B → C → A if the argument is that revenue stability must precede locational reform. Reject any order placing A before C, or anything before D.
